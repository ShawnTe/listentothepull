About your facilitators

It began in 2015, on a front porch in the Bay Area.

A big, beautiful Craftsman home — because of course there were flowers blooming everywhere and coffee involved.

Shawn and Simone were both in the middle of major life transitions, and every day they would sit outside, mugs in hand, doing what they now realize was sacred work before they had language for it:

  • Telling the truth
  • Reflecting life back
  • Harvesting the nuggets
  • Integrating the lessons
  • Laughing
  • Challenging each other
  • Asking the deeper questions

Simone

From that front porch, Simone’s spiral led her to finding love at Burning Man, marriage, motherhood and deeper devotion to the art of holding transformational space.

That thread had begun in 2002, when she fell in love with participatory facilitation through the Art of Hosting — spaces where wisdom lives in the room, authentic truth can emerge, and people remember they belong.

Over the next two decades, she designed and held spaces around the world, from the European Parliament, the World Bank, ministries of education, Homeboy Industries, Burning Man organization, several tech start-ups and communities navigating conflict, repair, and change.

With an MA in Leadership and training as a conflict mediator, she became the one people called for the dicey conversations: money, race, power, belonging, truth.

All along, she was tending the intuitive, earth-based parts of herself too: tarot, ancestors, meditation, moon cycles, and the Celtic wheel of the year.

Today, Simone brings the ritual, magic, and deep holding of the space in between that help people evolve into the next version of themselves while honoring what’s ending, sensing what’s shifting, and trusting what’s ready to emerge.

Shawn

Meanwhile, Shawn was walking her own spiral. Before tech, before software engineering, before the move to San Diego, before the running shoes and guitar strings and reinventions, she spent seven years as a mindful productivity coach and content creator — helping people make their days, dreams, and nervous systems work together.

She was never interested in productivity for productivity’s sake. She was interested in the deeper question:

How do you live what you say matters?

That question followed her into tech, into engineering, into new cities, new identities, new disciplines, and eventually back to the Bay Area — and back into regular rhythm with Simone.

This year, she left tech to return to the thread that had been there all along: helping people who’ve felt an inner pull for too long stop waiting for certainty, trust themselves more deeply, and act on the change they’ve been sensing.
